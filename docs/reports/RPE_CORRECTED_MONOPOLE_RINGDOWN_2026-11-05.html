The corrected RPE echo: from ring-down to fitted dynamics

The error was in the observation, not the Rayleigh–Plesset equation. A missing radius factor overstated the collapse echo. We retained the five physical trajectories and recomputed their measurements, the frozen history basis, vector-field fits and the joint radius–echo observer. The September and October campaigns were audited, not rerun.

What changed in the physics?

The observable is the spherical far-field pressure times receiver distance, evaluated at source (retarded) time: ρ(2RṘ² + R²R̈), in Pa·m. This is not pressure in Pa at a specified receiver. DynSysLab uses the zero-input acceleration for the intrinsic sensor: it is the actual acceleration on the unforced training trajectory; directly scattered driving pressure is outside this model. See the far-field radiation relation, Eq. 6.

r = R/R₀, v = dr/dτ, a = dv/dτ, S = R₀P* = 0.1099790787846 Pa·m
Old: e = S(2rv² + ra)       Corrected: e = S(2rv² + r²a)
Corrected e = 1.1217/r³ − 0.9767r − 0.145 − 0.0132785226733v + 0.0549895393923rv²

R₀ = 10 µm, ρ = 998 kg/m³, μ = 0.001 Pa·s, σ = 0.0725 N/m, κ = 4/3, ambient pressure = 100,000 Pa, vapor pressure = 2,330 Pa. One τ = 3.01238330 µs. Unforced training IC: (2.3333865986, 0), τ = 0…80, Δτ = 0.0001, 800,001 samples. Echo histories, rebuilt

Only the corrected scalar echo of the main IC entered the SVD. Same 12,751 samples per window (1.275 τ), RMS scaling, decimation 1, seed 20261104, oversampling 16, 32 subspace iterations, rank 8. The first two modes now contain 30.65% of total Hankel energy, versus 8.25% previously; the first eight contain 58.27%, versus 17.84%. The denominator includes all modes, not just the extracted eight.

ξⱼ(k) = Uⱼᵀ[e(k−12750),…,e(k)]/σⱼ in native units. The frozen basis was applied to all five records. No second SVD or future sample enters that projection. The two native projection paths agreed exactly on all 787,251 training windows; 29 independent dot-product checks differed by at most 8.33×10⁻¹⁷. Energy concentration alone does not establish injectivity or Markov closure.

Does the ambiguity occur on the actual data?

Yes, including two different times on the same training trajectory. The closest coarse-grid matches were refined in time on cubic Hermite interpolants of the native samples; the native observable and both derivatives were then evaluated at the interpolated physical states. These are numerical crossings, not assertions that two saved sample times are exactly identical.
