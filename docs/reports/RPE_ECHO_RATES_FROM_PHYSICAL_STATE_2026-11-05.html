RPE echo derivatives from radius and radial velocity

The inputs are (r,v), where v = dr/dτ. The two outputs are e′ and e″, both differentiated with respect to τ. This direct map is single-valued on the physical domain r > 0. The corrected unforced sensor gives e in Pa·m. All results use the high-resolution viscous air-in-water ring-down at R₀ = 10 µm, κ = 4/3, μ = 0.001 Pa·s and vapor pressure = 2330 Pa.

a = 10.199212545 r⁻⁵ − 8.880779970 r⁻¹ − 1.318432575 r⁻² − 0.120736806 v r⁻² − 1.5 v² r⁻¹
e = 1.1217 r⁻³ − 0.9767r − 0.145 − 0.0132785226733v + 0.0549895393923rv²
L = v ∂/∂r + a ∂/∂v
e′ = Le,   e″ = L(Le)

Applying L term by term produces the analytic columns below. The expanded expressions agree with the registered native observable derivatives on all 800,001 samples of each scored IC to relative RMS below 10⁻¹². Write s = 1/r and sum each coefficient times its monomial to read the equations.

What SINDy selected

The larger Laurent library offers every product rᵖvᑫ with p from −9 to 1 and q from 0 to 4, 55 candidates per output. The exponent range was chosen to contain the RPE expansion. The algorithm selects the individual powers separately for each output. STLS prunes after feature and response RMS scaling, using the same SVD relative cutoff 10⁻¹² throughout. The standard pruning threshold is 10⁻³ and the low-threshold sensitivity check uses 10⁻⁸. A threshold of zero in the fixed-term fits gives least squares on the supplied term list.
