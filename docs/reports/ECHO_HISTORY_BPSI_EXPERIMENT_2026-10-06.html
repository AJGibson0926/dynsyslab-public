Echo-history Bψ regression and KLQR control

Compiled C++ experiments, with the existing solver, eigenfunction fitting, numerical regression, LQR and Viewer components.

Outcome: partial success for the mild target, no success for the nonlinear target. The best short-history, two-latent trial achieved 8.88% late-time echo error and 21.61% driver error. This is approximate tracking, not accurate recovery of the known driver, and does not establish a generally reliable control map.

What was tested

76 forced calibration recordings (60 broad-band combinations and 16 frequency-local runs), 9 held-out forced recordings, 8 new unforced training recordings and 6 unforced validation recordings, and 2 sine-driven targets. The existing free-trained ψ was compared with free-only polynomial refits in two and four latent coordinates. There were 104 KLQR attempts; 6 did not produce a complete saved result, including 4 NPZ write failures when the disk filled. No failed attempt is omitted below. Storage failures are incomplete experiments, not evidence of controller instability.

A low one-step prediction error is not sufficient evidence of control. Judge echo tracking, recovery of the known driver, long-time behavior and saturation together. Best runs below are selected retrospectively from a parameter sweep; they are exploratory comparisons, not untouched confirmation tests.

The information boundary

Echo history → ξ → ψ(ξ), B̂ψ(ξ) → existing KLQR → applied pressure

The simulator alone knows the RPE equations and physical state. It writes separate measurement-only archives containing time, intrinsic echo and applied input. The regression reader rejects state-bearing archives. The history transform, eigenfunction fitting, Bψ fitting and controller receive no r, ṙ, state Jacobians or equations of motion. The target is supplied as an echo recording transformed through the same basis and polynomial. Its recorded input is withheld from the control calculation and used only for the evaluation metrics and overlays.

Physical states in the plots are post-hoc simulator truth, not controller inputs. Driver scattering, direct acoustic feedthrough, dipoles and higher multipoles are intentionally excluded, as agreed. No sensor noise, actuator dynamics or laboratory uncertainty is included.

Bubble, data and targets

The source KROM fixes the air–water RPE parameters: equilibrium radius 10 μm, ambient pressure 100 kPa, viscosity 0.001 Pa·s, density 998 kg/m³, surface tension 0.0725 N/m, vapour pressure 2330 Pa and κ = 4/3. No parameter was retuned.

The broad calibration grid uses amplitudes 0.003, 0.01, 0.03, 0.08, 0.2 and 0.4 in p/p₀, frequencies 0.65, 0.85, 1.0, 1.15 and 1.35 cycles/τ, and phases 0 and π/2. Each drive lasts 16 cycles, with a 6τ unforced prefix and 6τ free tail. Nine held-out runs use new amplitudes, frequencies, phase 0.37 and initial radius 1.005. The local calibration uses 32 cycles at each target frequency, different amplitudes and phases 0.4 and 1.1; its fits exclude the first 6τ of the driven segment. It is frequency-local calibration, not frequency-blind generalization.

The mild target is 1.2 kPa at 0.93 cycles/τ (about 309 kHz), and the nonlinear target is 30 kPa at 0.83 cycles/τ (about 276 kHz). Both start at equilibrium and run to 60τ, with driving from 6τ onward. The nonlinear reference has r ≈ 0.573–1.478 and ṙ ≈ −3.900–3.849, a substantial nonlinear oscillation. The mild reference has r ≈ 0.967–1.033.

Sensor sampling is Δτ = 0.0005. Applied sine inputs are sampled and zero-order held for 0.01τ, about 100 actuator samples per natural period. This is a numerical sampled-sine experiment, not an exact continuously varying actuator. The final 30τ supplies the long-time error metrics. Inputs are clipped to ±0.6 p/p₀.

How ψ was fitted

The original basis has 8,644 samples spanning 4.3215τ; the original polynomial is degree five in ξ₁–ξ₂ and was fitted over τ = 50–80. For the first refits its U and singular values were frozen. New free training IC radii are 0.65, 0.8, 0.9, 1.05, 1.2, 1.4, 1.7 and 2.0, all with zero initial velocity. Six separate free validation radii are 0.7, 0.85, 1.1, 1.3, 1.55 and 1.85.

The existing pooled polynomial EDMD routine fits snapshot pairs 0.01τ apart, with relative ridge 10⁻¹⁰. Candidates use starts 5, 15, 35 or 50τ and degrees 1, 3 or 5 for two latents; four latents use degrees 1 or 3. Candidate modes require −0.5 ≤ Re λ ≤ −0.001 and 4 ≤ Im λ ≤ 7. Each is stored in the normal polynomial/SINDy eigenfunction contract; these coefficients come from EDMD, not sparse thresholded generator-PDE fitting.

Selection minimizes the sum of the mean held-out 1τ and 4τ semigroup relative-L₂ errors, always evaluated over τ = 5–80, including early motion even when fitting starts later. This is validation-based selection, not an independent test of the selected ψ. The additional 0.05τ basis uses 101 samples and is fitted only to the eight unforced records by the existing pooled Hankel routine. Neither its basis nor any eigenfunction is fitted to forced data.

How Bψ was regressed and used

At each actuator tick the recorded echo history gives zₖ = ψ(ξₖ). With μ = exp(λh), the regression target is qₖ = zₖ₊₁ − μzₖ. Least squares fits qₖ ≈ B̂d(ξₖ)uₖ. No numerical echo derivative or physical-state derivative is required. Constant maps and the existing conjugate-complete bilinear family are compared:

B̂d(ξ) = c₀ + c₁ Re ψ(ξ) + c₂ Im ψ(ξ).

This is a restricted function of the latent coordinates, through ψ; it is not an unrestricted polynomial in every ξ component. The design columns are normalized before the existing least-squares solve, with ridge 10⁻⁸. Whole runs are kept separate; no pair crosses a recording boundary. Small-amplitude and pooled fits are distinct comparisons.

For the existing continuous-time KLQR solver, the fitted discrete map is converted as B̂ψ = B̂d / G, where G = (exp(λh) − 1)/λ. For a varying B this is a frozen-map, zero-order-hold approximation over h, not an exact instantaneous Jacobian. The maps are saved using native ConstantControlMap or BilinearControlMap array conventions.

The controller evaluates the current polynomial and fitted map at each tick, calls the existing solveContinuousLQR routine, and applies u = −K[Re(z − z*), Im(z − z*)]ᵀ. There is no feedforward term and no use of the known target driver. Q = I, and R = α·RMS(z*)²/0.1²; α is swept and recorded per trial. Stronger feedback is not assumed to be better.

Interpretation and limits

The original nonlinear target is outside the old two-latent training bounding box for 93.64% of available history windows. The mild target is entirely inside that box and still exhibits substantial errors. Thus polynomial extrapolation matters, but cannot alone explain every failure. Being inside a bounding box also does not prove that a forced history is covered by the manifold of unforced histories.

A history projection can predict unforced evolution better after a ψ refit and still fail to provide a closed forced model ż = λz + Bψ(ξ)u. Past actuation can change the history while producing similar retained coordinates. The comparisons are consistent with that issue, but do not independently prove it: restricted map expressiveness, the unforced eigenfunction approximation, control gain/sampling and the continuous-time conversion are additional limitations. These results must not be interpreted as a proof that echo-only control is impossible.

The next scientifically discriminating test is matched-history input perturbations and conditional residual analysis: determine whether different histories with similar ξ require different B values. If so, enrich the observation coordinates or represent input memory before demanding more from the same regression. If not, a richer Bψ(ξ) regression may suffice. No production controller replacement is justified by these experiments.

Reproducibility and local assets

All scientific computation is C++. Python only assembles these native result tables and Viewer payloads. No existing completed archive was overwritten; large datasets and plots remain local and Git-ignored.

Sources: native/tools/echo_input/, native/src/HistoryInputRegression.cpp and native/include/dynsyslab/core/HistoryInputRegression.hpp. The existing causal projector gained append/projectLatest methods so acquisition can run faster than control. Production LQR.cpp and the existing physical-state regression pipeline were not modified.

Native executable: build/native-suite-fast/bin/dynsyslab_echo_input_experiment.exe. Supply the frozen KROM path and a fresh output directory for the base run, then use --refine, --eigenfunctions, and --short-history in that order. Completed results are immutable.
