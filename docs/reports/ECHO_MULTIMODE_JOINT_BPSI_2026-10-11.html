1. Precisely what was refitted

Each window has three fixed complex eigenfunctions ψ1, ψ2, ψ3 of the same two latent coordinates (ξ₁, ξ₂). For every nonempty subset S = {1}, {2}, {3}, {1,2}, {1,3}, {2,3}, {1,2,3}, every coefficient of every selected B row was refitted from the calibration records, including cross-mode predictors. The resulting 28 separate maps cover 2 windows × 2 calibration regimes × 7 subsets. Penalty changes reuse the map for that set; penalties do not affect regression.

zⱼ,ₖ = ψⱼ(ξ₁,ₖ, ξ₂,ₖ),   μⱼ = exp(λⱼh),   qⱼ,ₖ = zⱼ,ₖ₊₁ − μⱼzⱼ,ₖ
qⱼ,ₖ ≈ [cⱼ₀ + Σℓ∈S(aⱼℓ Re zℓ,ₖ + bⱼℓ Im zℓ,ₖ)]uₖ,   j ∈ S.

For m complex eigenfunctions, the real design matrix has 1+2m columns: u times a constant, then the real and imaginary parts of all selected modes. A simultaneous least-squares solve fits 2m real output columns. It is equivalent to separate output-row regressions sharing the same expanded design—not an additional constraint tying their coefficients together. Columns are normalized by their L² norms; the existing native SVD least-squares routine uses ridge 10⁻⁸. The same single-mode calculation agrees with the existing history-input fitter. This is not a constant B matrix: B is evaluated as an affine function of the selected eigenfunction values, hence ultimately of echo-history coordinates.

The map is stored in the existing conjugate-complete BilinearControlMap: Bcⱼ(z) = βⱼ₀ + Σ(Aⱼℓzℓ + Cⱼℓconj(zℓ)). Discrete coefficients are divided by Iⱼ = (exp(λⱼh)−1)/λⱼ for use in the unchanged continuous controller. This preserves the previous frozen-map sampled-to-continuous approximation; it is not claimed to be exact sampled-data LQR.

3. What the refit improves—and what it does not

For short/mild ψ2, the old self-only B row had 102.67% withheld-waveform residual. In the jointly refitted pair, it is 38.66%; ψ1's row is 17.51%. The pair's training residuals are 11.58% and 30.16%. Its scaled design is full rank (5/5), condition 1.92. Cross-mode predictors meaningfully improve the fitted forced-response relationship; this was missing from the earlier comparison.

In the short/mild triple, the held-out residuals are 17.97%, 46.68%, and 82.39%. The third free-trained eigenfunction already has roughly 105% late 4-τ semigroup error. More polynomial features do not guarantee trustworthy extra controlled dynamics. The increased model capacity helps some rows, but the third controlled output is still poorly modeled and its feedback can distort the driver.

For the short/nonlinear triple, the scaled design condition is about 273,017. Held-out residuals are approximately 68%, yet the target-only residuals are orders of magnitude larger. This is severe extrapolation/model failure, not a small tuning error. Full numerical rank alone does not make a regression useful.

4. Revised conclusion and boundaries

  • Use the jointly refitted short-window ψ1+ψ2 case as the next mild-case experimental reference. It improves both echo tracking and driver recovery over the single fundamental at the same penalty and over the weight-matched single.
  • Do not assume three eigenfunctions are better than two. Here the third harms driver recovery despite low echo error. Different choices of modes, more robust free validity and different weighting remain separate questions.
  • The long-window and nonlinear failures remain. Refitting B corrects an important limitation of the earlier test, but does not remove forced-history closure errors, extrapolation, numerical conditioning or lack of independent information in ξ₁,ξ₂.
  • Extra eigenfunctions remain functions of the same two measurements-derived coordinates. They cannot separate histories that already project to the same ξ₁,ξ₂. This run does not test four latent variables or a new history length.
  • All feedback laws still vanish at zero lifted tracking error. Without target feedforward, exact sustained tracking of a trajectory requiring nonzero forcing is not generally invariant. This controller limitation is held fixed, not repaired here.
  • Of 100 attempts (84 main-grid and 16 weight checks), 85 completed and 15 failed with the unchanged CARE solver reporting a non-real Riccati solution. These are numerical failures, not evidence of physical uncontrollability. Completed runs can also be numerically suspect: all maximum CARE residuals and saturation fractions are retained below.
  • Repeated use of the same validation records and the same two targets makes this a controlled diagnostic comparison, not a broad generalization certificate. No new independent robustness/noise test was performed.

The core pipeline was not modified. No models were promoted into it. A conservative next step would be independent mild-target and perturbation validation of the refitted pair, with the same measurement-only causal diagnostics, before claiming general control capability.
