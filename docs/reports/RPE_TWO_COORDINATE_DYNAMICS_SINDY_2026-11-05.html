What the experiment establishes

Differentiation is accurate. Candidate choice and sparse model selection matter much more here.

The compact inverse-radius library recovers the raw-state RPE law extremely accurately. Fourth-order polynomials fail for this strongly nonlinear ring-down. The broad library contains the correct law too, but an overly aggressive sparsity threshold discarded an important term and produced a worse model.

The echo and two-latent-coordinate fits do not establish reliable closed dynamics. Echo models transfer poorly, and the broad latent fit retains all 99 candidates while missing sharp derivative features. These results alone do not prove non-injectivity: library error, conditioning, sparsification, sampling coverage and coordinate closure must be distinguished.

No eigenfunctions or controllers are trained here. Existing Koopman SINDy and its options are unchanged.

Why was the broad library worse if it contains the inverse-radius library?

The objection was correct. Every nonzero compact-model term was matched structurally to the broad expression dictionary. The linear velocity term was converted into the broad dictionary’s affine normalization. Native evaluation of this embedded model reproduces the compact model across training and both validation records at approximately 10⁻¹⁶ relative RMS.

The original comparison also changed settings: the compact fit used threshold 0.001 and fitting stride 4; the broad fit used threshold 0.01 and stride 10. It was not a controlled comparison of dictionary size alone. The following checks hold the rows fixed at stride 4 and isolate pruning, numerical rank tolerance, and derivative source.

Before pruning, the broad library achieves about 6.6×10⁻⁸ residual against the sampled acceleration derivatives. At threshold 0.01, support decreases from 117 to 54 to 41 to 25 columns. The last reduction increases residual from about 2.1×10⁻⁷ to 1.34×10⁻³. That final model has discarded the exact viscous term v/r², despite its presence in the dictionary. It uses large compensating coefficients in correlated alternatives.

This is a sparse-selection failure, not a missing-function failure. Sequential thresholding does not find the globally optimal sparse support. A redundant dictionary can distribute contributions among correlated terms, making individual coefficient size a misleading measure of importance.

Restoring threshold 0.001, with identical samples and the original SVD cutoff, reduces exact acceleration error to about 1.45×10⁻⁶. The compact fit is about 3.49×10⁻⁷. The dense broad fit follows numerical differentiation error more closely; the compact model denoises it. Least squares minimizes the supplied derivative-target error, not error against the exact equations. Using exact derivative targets with no pruning gives a broad-library acceleration error of approximately 1.9×10⁻¹¹.

2. Echo jet: q=(e,w), with w=e′

This is the exact registered echo expression used in the existing archives, expanded from SystemsRPE.cpp. Its chain-rule reference was checked against the saved coordinate derivatives.

e = h(r,v) = S[A/r4 − B − C/r − Dv/r + (2r−1.5)v2]

w = hrv + hva
e′ = w
w′ = hrrv2 + 2hrvva + hvva2 + hra + hv(arv + ava)

These are exact rates evaluated through (r,v). If H(r,v)=(h,Lfh) has an inverse on a region, the intrinsic equation there is (e′,w′)=(w,(Lf2h)∘H−1(e,w)). This experiment did not construct that inverse. Where multiple states have the same echo jet, a closed law requires their corresponding rates also to agree.

The numerical-derivative polynomial SINDy fit uses z₁=(e−0.3038197088855248)/4.549588759044878 and z₂=(w−0.013215063063321417)/1223.3699921474044. Its first equation is e′≈1223.368254655184 z₂. The second is the full polynomial listed below. The broad echo equation uses 37 acceleration terms, also listed below.

The unconstrained fit does not preserve e′=w exactly: its constant correction was pruned after centering. Even the polynomial fit with exact derivative targets gives approximately e′=w−0.0132150631. This is a fitting artifact, not a modification to the exact chain rule. No coefficient has been silently corrected in this report.

3. Frozen echo-history coordinates: q=(ξ₁,ξ₂)

The saved U rows are ordered from oldest to newest. Define m=12,751, Δτ=0.0001, dℓ=(m−1−ℓ)Δτ, and weights bℓj=Uℓj/σj. The two retained modes have j=1,2.

ξj(τ) = Σℓ=0m−1 bℓj e(τ−dℓ)
ξ′j(τ) = Σℓ=0m−1 bℓj [hrv+hva]τ−dℓ

This is an exact derivative of the frozen history projection along the smooth simulated signal. It depends on the full history. We have not obtained an exact closed analytic ξ′=fξ(ξ₁,ξ₂). The SINDy equations below are attempts to approximate such a closure. Two retained numbers do not automatically determine the history-dependent rate.

The polynomial latent fit uses z₁=(ξ₁−0.00016630196648901357)/0.005136338704172038 and z₂=(ξ₂−0.00017616339221099625)/0.005136020149146403. Both fitted equations include terms through total degree four. The broad latent model retains all 99 terms in each equation. Its large cancelling coefficients and poor validation mean it is not an established accurate two-dimensional dynamical model.

The two derivative routes

x = (r,v),   ẋ = f(x)
q = (h(x), Lfh(x)),   q̇ = (Lfh(x), Lf²h(x))
ξj(τ) = Σℓ Uℓj e(τ−delayℓ)/σj
ξ̇j(τ) = Σℓ Uℓj ė(τ−delayℓ)/σj

Exact references come from the registered RPE equations and observable expressions. The other route differentiates sampled coordinates with the centered five-point fourth-order formula, then fits their vector field. Two endpoint samples are trimmed on each side; no endpoint rates are invented.

The echo-jet coordinate itself uses the existing analytic echo-dot observable, so that case is not a wholly measurement-only coordinate construction. The latent coordinate fit needs only the synthetic echo history; echo-dot is used solely for its optional derivative reference.

The chain rule supplies rates along the original trajectory. Calling them a globally defined fq(q) additionally requires an inverse or consistent derivatives on every fiber of the observation map. Two projected history variables are not automatically a closed two-dimensional system.

Why global RMS and attractive phase portraits are insufficient

The polynomial echo fit has about 10.4% full-record second-derivative error, yet its late-time error (τ=25…80) exceeds 9,300%. Collapse bursts dominate the global normalization. Conversely, latent fits can miss narrow derivative spikes even where a smooth phase loop looks convincing. The broad latent fit is dense, and its τ=1.28 rollout fails.
