# RPE echo-jet KLQR control-signal derivation

Date: 2026-09-15  
System: dimensionless Rayleigh-Plesset equation (RPE), 10 micrometer air
bubble in water at 20 C  
Cases: 100 kHz pure-sine targets at 20 kPa and 40 kPa

## Executive answer

The two reported pressure signals were generated by closed-loop KLQR feedback.
They were **not** copied from the known sine drivers, obtained by fitting a sine
to the target, or supplied as feedforward inputs.

For each accepted solver step, the controller:

1. maps the current RPE state into the two observable coordinates
   `[echo, autonomous echo-dot]`;
2. evaluates one learned complex Koopman eigenfunction in those coordinates;
3. maps the archived target state at the current time through the same
   observable and eigenfunction maps;
4. evaluates the exact, state-dependent lifted input direction;
5. solves a local continuous-time Riccati problem; and
6. applies feedback proportional to the lifted-coordinate tracking error.

The known sine stored with each target archive was withheld from this path. It
was read only after the controlled solve to assess whether the emergent feedback
signal reproduced the driver that originally generated the target motion.

The same trained echo-jet eigenfunction and the same selected Koopman mode were
used in both cases. The 20 kPa case used `Q=5000`, `R=0.0001`. The more nonlinear
40 kPa orbit required a softer input weight, `Q=5000`, `R=0.006`, to avoid
switching to a competing echo-coordinate feedback branch.

Over `tau=100..300`, the 20 kPa controller recovered a 20.215 kPa fundamental
with 2.116% full-waveform NRMSE and 0.999838 correlation. The 40 kPa controller
recovered a 39.477 kPa fundamental with 18.860% full-waveform NRMSE and 0.982380
correlation. The larger error in the 40 kPa signal is real: it consists mainly
of repeatable, phase-locked correction spikes around an otherwise accurate
sinusoidal fundamental.

## What “rediscovered the sine driver” means

The target trajectory was generated offline from a known pressure,

\[
  p_*(t)=A\sin(2\pi(100\ \mathrm{kHz})t+\pi/2),
\]

with `A=20 kPa` or `40 kPa`. With ambient pressure `p0=100 kPa` as the
input scale and model time `tau=t/T`, this is

\[
  u_*(\tau)=a\cos(1.8927362514011978\,\tau),
  \qquad a\in\{0.2,0.4\}.
\]

The solve jobs point only to the target archive's `time` and `values` arrays.
Although `control_values` is retained in the immutable target archive for
provenance, the controller configuration does not name or load it. Therefore
“rediscovery” means that feedback needed to keep the unforced model on the
prescribed orbit converged to nearly the same pressure waveform.

This is closed-loop recovery, not open-loop system identification. In
particular, when the controlled state initially equals the target state, the
pure tracking law initially requests zero input. The target then begins to move
away under its hidden forcing, a lifted tracking error develops, and feedback
generates the compensating pressure. This helps explain the acquisition
transient and the small settled waveform bias.

## 1. Plant and input convention

The state is

\[
  x=(r,v),\qquad r=R/R_0,\qquad v=dr/d\tau,
\]

with `R0=10 micrometers` and
`T=3.0123833038 microseconds`. The archived dimensionless plant is

\[
\begin{aligned}
  \dot r &= v,\\
  \dot v &=
  \frac{G r^{-4}+V-P-C/r-Dv/r-\tfrac32v^2-Pu}{r},
\end{aligned}
\]

where

```text
G = 10.1992125448       gas-pressure ratio
V = 0.2118584758        vapor-pressure ratio
P = 9.0926384459        ambient-pressure ratio
C = 1.3184325747        capillarity
D = 0.1207368058        viscosity
```

The input vector is

\[
  g(x)=\begin{bmatrix}0 & -P/r\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Thus positive `u` is positive applied acoustic pressure and enters the radial
acceleration with a negative sign, compressing the bubble.

## 2. First identification stage: obtain a useful Koopman spectrum

The spectral lineage begins with a broad, unforced RPE transient. It contains
25,001 samples over `tau=0..25`, with `dtau=0.001`, starting
from `[2.3333866,0]`. Its ranges, `r=0.232721..2.333387` and
`v=-27.0932..26.9480`, make it useful for spectral excitation but too broad
for a globally well-conditioned echo-coordinate polynomial model.

Hankel DMD was applied to radius using a 20,000-sample window, 2,000 delay
coordinates, stride one, projected DMD, and retained rank 2,000. This generated
the candidate discrete spectrum. A degree-four SINDy eigenfunction fit in the
physical state coordinates `(r,v)` then solved

\[
  \Gamma c\simeq\lambda\Theta c,
  \qquad \Gamma=\nabla\Theta\,f_0,
\]

for all 14 nonconstant monomials through total degree four. A compact extraction
selected energy-ordered source mode 1 and its conjugate. The retained continuous
eigenvalue pair is

\[
  \lambda=-0.2106164458\pm4.8186345616i.
\]

This first KROM supplies the spectral pair used later. It is not the final
echo-coordinate controller model.

## 3. Second training set: cover the branch that will be controlled

The first free transient exposed a key failure: even `[echo, echo-dot]` has
near-self-intersections and near-rank loss over that violent global domain. A
single normalized degree-four fit there retained approximately 100% relative
generator defect.

To identify a useful local chart, the compact state-coordinate KROM drove the
RPE along a moderate multisine state target,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_{ref}(\tau)&=1+0.09\sin(4\tau)-0.12\sin(3\tau),\\
 v_{ref}(\tau)&=0.36\cos(4\tau)-0.36\cos(3\tau).
\end{aligned}
\]

That forced, branch-covering trajectory provides 10,001 samples over
`tau=0..10` and spans approximately
`r=0.7975..1.2022`, `v=-0.6444..0.7114`. It is training data for the
echo-coordinate refit; neither sine target is part of this fit.

![Forced branch-covering training trajectory](../../viewer_outputs/rpe-echo-jet2-branch-training-evidence.png)

## 4. Refit the eigenfunction in echo and autonomous echo-dot

The final coordinate map is

\[
  H(x)=\begin{bmatrix}e(x)\\e_1(x)\end{bmatrix}
      =\begin{bmatrix}h(x)\\L_{f_0}h(x)\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The far-field echo observable is

\[
  e=R_0P_s r\left(2v^2+\frac{Z}{r}\right),
\]

where `Z` is the autonomous RPE momentum numerator. The second coordinate is
the exact symbolic Lie derivative of this echo through the zero-input vector
field. Both the coordinate values and their state Jacobian are therefore
analytic.

The training coordinates were standardized using

```text
center = [ 0.1472323322, -0.0894546951 ]
scale  = [ 0.5530497264,  1.9641872085 ]
```

and the same 14-term degree-four polynomial library was fit while reusing the
parent KROM's retained eigenvalue pair. The postprocess job explicitly sets
`include_input_terms=false`, so its generator target is the autonomous drift
`f0`, not the closed-loop field that produced the branch-covering states.

The resulting fit has 3.779% relative generator defect, 0.999288
generator-target alignment, median standardized observation-Jacobian condition
1.451, and maximum 2.786 on its training branch. The plot below shows the
Hankel-DMD spectrum from which the pair was retained, the normalized
echo-coordinate chart, the learned eigenfunction along that chart, and the
measured-versus-predicted generator action. The closure plot's dashed identity
lines make the remaining fit defect visible without reducing it to a filename.

![Spectrum selection and echo-jet eigenfunction fit](../../viewer_outputs/rpe-echo-jet2-spectrum-fit-evidence.png)

## 5. Lifted coordinates and exact input direction

Let `eta=(H(x)-center)/scale`. The learned complex eigenfunction is a sparse
polynomial

\[
  \psi(x)=\phi(H(x))=\sum_{|\alpha|=1}^{4}c_\alpha\eta^\alpha.
\]

Only mode index 0 is selected. One complex mode already supplies the two real
controller coordinates

\[
  z(x)=\begin{bmatrix}\Re\psi(x)\\\Im\psi(x)\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Selecting its stored conjugate separately would duplicate this real subspace.
For `lambda=alpha+i beta`, the autonomous lifted matrix is

\[
  A=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The final jobs use the exact state-dependent lifted input direction

\[
  B_z(x)=J_z(H(x))\,J_H(x)\,g(x).
\]

This expression is recomputed from the current plant state; no constant or
bilinear approximation to `B_z` was substituted.

## 6. How each instantaneous control value is produced

At model time `tau`, the controller performs the following calculation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 y       &= H(x(\tau)),\\
 z       &= \operatorname{realSplit}(\phi(y)),\\
 y_{ref} &= H(x_{ref}(\tau)),\\
 z_{ref} &= \operatorname{realSplit}(\phi(y_{ref})),\\
 B       &= B_z(x(\tau)).
\end{aligned}
\]

It then solves the local continuous-time algebraic Riccati equation

\[
  A^{\mathsf T}S+SA-SBR^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}S+Q=0,
\]

forms

\[
  K=R^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}S,
\]

and applies

\[
  u(\tau)=-K\bigl(z(x(\tau))-z_{ref}(\tau)\bigr).
\]

There is no `u_*` term in this equation. The pressure trace archived as
`control_values` is the sequence of these feedback evaluations during the BDF
integration of the physical RPE. Control is evaluated at continuous RHS calls,
not merely at the saved output times.

## 7. Target construction and the two final jobs

### 20 kPa low-amplitude case

The target is a 100 kHz, 20 kPa sine-driven trajectory from `[1,0]`. The final
controlled run also starts from `[1,0]`, runs to `tau=300`, publishes at
`0.02 tau`, and uses BDF with `rtol=atol=1e-9` and maximum internal step
`0.001 tau`.

```text
Q = 5000
R = 0.0001
selected_modes = [0]
control_representation = exact
```

The full run contains a brief unconstrained acquisition command of 28.606 model
units, or 2.861 MPa. That peak is not visible in the final-six-cycle figure and
is not actuator-feasible. By `tau=20`, the controller has settled to the correct
periodic branch.

### 40 kPa nonlinear case

The 40 kPa target was first integrated to `tau=300`. A phase-consistent state
from its settled attractor,

```text
[r,v] = [0.8875145511136061, -0.03863871558543898],
```

was used to start both a fresh target archive and the controlled run. This
removes target spin-up as a confound. The final job publishes every `0.01 tau`
and otherwise uses the same BDF tolerances and maximum internal step.

```text
Q = 5000
R = 0.006
selected_modes = [0]
control_representation = exact
```

The original 20 kPa penalty `R=0.0001` failed on this orbit by repeatedly
selecting a competing feedback branch. A gain sweep identified branch-stable
soft penalties. `R=0.006` was retained because it remained locked for all 59
complete cycles after `tau=100` and was more accurate than the stable
`R=0.01` comparator.

The controlled solve itself still did not read the known sine. However, the
human selection of `R=0.006` used post-run driver error and long-horizon branch
stability. The 40 kPa result is therefore a held-out-target test of the KROM,
but not a blind hyperparameter-selection experiment.

## 9. What the result establishes—and what it does not

The evidence establishes that the locally repaired `[echo,L_f0 echo]`
eigenfunction can support long-term orbit tracking and recover the driver's
dominant sinusoid on held-out 20 and 40 kPa targets. It also establishes that
the 40 kPa case is much closer to a folded or weakly conditioned part of the
learned feedback geometry.

It does **not** yet establish a laboratory echo-only controller:

1. Under control, a measured echo derivative is
   `de/dtau = L_f0 e + L_g e u`, not the autonomous coordinate `L_f0 e` used by
   the KROM. A real estimator must subtract or model the known input term.
2. The exact `B_z(x)` implementation evaluates the simulated physical state.
   A sensor-side implementation still needs an input-aware state/coordinate
   observer or a separately validated observable-only input map.
3. Neither test includes echo noise, derivative filtering error, parameter
   mismatch, sampling delay, transducer bandwidth, or pressure saturation.
4. The 20 kPa acquisition peak and 40 kPa correction spikes are incompatible
   with an unconstrained laboratory actuator design. Constrained KMPC,
   saturation-aware acquisition, or scheduled handoff into local KLQR is the
   appropriate next controller layer.
