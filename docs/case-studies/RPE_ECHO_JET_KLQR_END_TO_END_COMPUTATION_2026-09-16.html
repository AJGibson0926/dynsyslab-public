RPE echo-jet KLQR: end-to-end computation audit

Date: 2026-09-16
System: dimensionless Rayleigh-Plesset equation, 10 micrometer air bubble in water at 20 C
Controlled cases: 100 kHz, 20 kPa and 40 kPa pure-sine target orbits

Direct answer: yes. The target supplied to the final controller contains both echo and autonomous echo-dot, in that order. For every target time, the archived reference state (r_ref,v_ref) is mapped to H(x_ref)=[e(x_ref),L_f0 e(x_ref)]. Both values are then passed through the learned polynomial eigenfunction. KLQR penalizes the resulting two real lifted-coordinate errors, not the two raw observable errors independently.

This report reconstructs that statement from the saved jobs, numerical arrays, and the native implementation. It also separates three ideas that are easy to conflate:

  1. the two-component coordinate chart used to fit the eigenfunction;
  2. the two-component mapped target supplied to the eigenfunction; and
  3. the two real KLQR states, Re(psi) and Im(psi), on which Q acts.

2. Physical state, time, and actuation

The model state is

\[ x=(r,v),\qquad r=R/R_0,\qquad v=dr/d\tau, \]

where R0=10 micrometers and one model-time unit is T=3.0123833038 microseconds. The plant is

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot r &= v,\\ \dot v &= \frac{Z(r,v)-P u}{r},\\ Z(r,v) &= G r^{-4}-\frac32v^2+V-P-\frac Cr-\frac{Dv}{r}. \end{aligned} \]

The control input is normalized by the 100 kPa ambient pressure. Therefore u=0.2 is 20 kPa and u=0.4 is 40 kPa. Its control-affine direction is

\[ g(x)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\-P/r\end{bmatrix}. \]

This sign convention makes positive pressure compress the bubble.

3. First training data: excite a broad spectrum

The first dataset is one unforced transient beginning at x(0)=[2.3333866,0] in dimensionless coordinates. The legacy source stores physical time, radius, and wall velocity; post-processing converts them explicitly to tau, r, and v without changing the source bytes. The converted training record has:

  • 25,001 samples over tau=0..25;
  • uniform Delta tau=0.001;
  • radius range 0.232721..2.333387; and
  • radial-velocity range -27.0932..26.9480.

This violent ring-down is useful because it excites many decaying oscillatory components. It is not a good global chart for the final echo-polynomial model: the mapping to [e,L_f0 e] folds and becomes poorly conditioned over this large domain.

4. Hankel-DMD supplies frequencies, not the final eigenfunction

Hankel-DMD is applied to the scalar radius history. A 20,000-sample window is embedded into 2,000 delay coordinates with stride one; projected DMD retains rank 2,000. The result is a candidate discrete spectrum mu_j.

The postprocessor converts each candidate to a continuous-time generator value

\[ \lambda_j=\frac{\log\mu_j}{\Delta\tau}. \]

Hankel-DMD therefore answers: which exponential rates and frequencies are present in the free ring-down? It does not yet produce the instantaneous echo/eigenfunction map used by control.

The candidate spectrum contains 2,000 eigenvalues. A state-coordinate SINDy stage next fits a polynomial eigenfunction for each candidate using (r,v) as its variables. Its library contains the 14 nonconstant monomials of total degree one through four. For candidate lambda, it solves for a coefficient vector in the approximate null space of

\[ \left(\Gamma-\lambda\Theta\right)c, \qquad \Gamma=\nabla_x\Theta(x) f_0(x). \]

An energy-ordered extraction keeps source mode 1 and its conjugate partner. The continuous pair is

\[ \lambda=-0.2106164458\pm4.8186345616i. \]

The imaginary part corresponds to about 254.6 kHz in physical time. It is not the later 100 kHz target frequency. The controller is therefore not recovering the driver by selecting a Koopman eigenvalue at 100 kHz.

This compact state-coordinate KROM is an intermediate tool. It is used to make better training data for the final echo-coordinate KROM.

5. Second training data: cover the branch to be controlled

The compact parent KROM drives the physical RPE along the moderate state target

\[ \begin{aligned} r_{ref}(\tau)&=1+0.09\sin(4\tau)-0.12\sin(3\tau),\\ v_{ref}(\tau)&=0.36\cos(4\tau)-0.36\cos(3\tau). \end{aligned} \]

The resulting forced trajectory contains 10,001 states over tau=0..10, with r=0.7975..1.2022 and v=-0.6444..0.7114. The actual trajectory, rather than the analytic target formula, becomes the second training set. It samples the moderate branch on which later control will operate.

Although this dataset was produced by feedback, the final SINDy refit sets include_input_terms=false. At each sampled state it evaluates the autonomous RPE vector field f0, not the control-modified velocity that happened to carry the trajectory there. The forcing chooses where the model is sampled; it does not redefine the autonomous Koopman generator being fitted.

6. What echo_jet2 actually means

The final coordinate observable is

\[ H(x)=\begin{bmatrix}e(x)\\e_1(x)\end{bmatrix}, \qquad e_1=L_{f_0}e. \]

The echo is the analytic far-field pressure-distance observable

\[ e=R_0P_s\left(2rv^2+Z(r,v)\right). \]

Its autonomous Lie derivative is evaluated from the state, not by finite differencing a saved echo trace:

\[ e_1=e_r v+e_v\frac{Z}{r}, \]

with

\[ \begin{aligned} e_r &=R_0P_s\left(2v^2-4Gr^{-5}+Cr^{-2}+Dvr^{-2}\right),\\ e_v &=R_0P_s\left(4rv-3v-D/r\right). \end{aligned} \]

Two distinctions matter:

  1. e1 is the autonomous quantity L_f0 e. Under control, the total measured derivative is de/dtau=L_f0 e+L_g e u.
  2. On a forced target orbit, e1(x_ref) is likewise not generally the time derivative of the target echo waveform. It is the autonomous derivative evaluated at the target state.

Thus “echo-dot” in this experiment is an instantaneous state observable with derivative units in the model clock. It is not a numerical differentiator in the feedback loop.

7. Fitting the final eigenfunction in the echo chart

The 10,001 branch samples are mapped to y=H(x). Their two components are standardized with

center = [ 0.1472323322, -0.0894546951 ]
scale  = [ 0.5530497264,  1.9641872085 ]

Let eta=(y-center)/scale. The final library is again the 14 nonconstant monomials of total degree at most four,

\[ \Theta(\eta)= [\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_1^2,\eta_1\eta_2,\ldots,\eta_2^4]. \]

Because the coordinate map is analytic, the training derivative is computed by the exact push-forward

\[ \dot y=J_H(x)f_0(x)=\begin{bmatrix}e_1(x)\\L_{f_0}^2e(x)\end{bmatrix}. \]

The feature derivative is

\[ \Gamma=\nabla_y\Theta(y)\dot y. \]

For the retained lambda, SINDy finds the normalized sparse vector c that minimizes the residual of

\[ (\Gamma-\lambda\Theta)c\simeq0. \]

The complex eigenfunction used by control is therefore

\[ \psi(x)=\phi(H(x))=\Theta\!\left(\frac{H(x)-center}{scale}\right)c. \]

The fit retains the conjugate pair in the archive, but the final controller selects only mode 0. One nonreal mode already becomes two real controller coordinates. Selecting its stored conjugate as a second control mode would duplicate the same real subspace.

The accepted refit has 3.779% relative generator defect and 0.999288 alignment with the generator target on this branch. Its median and maximum standardized observation-Jacobian condition numbers are 1.451 and 2.786.

8. Exactly how the target is constructed

The 20 and 40 kPa targets are physical RPE trajectories generated beforehand with

\[ u_*(\tau)=a\cos(1.8927362514\tau), \qquad a=0.2\ \text{or}\ 0.4. \]

The KLQR jobs identify the target as space="observable" and kind="from_state". They name only the target archive's time and state arrays. At a controller evaluation time, the implementation performs

\[ x_{ref}(\tau) \longrightarrow y_{ref}(\tau)=H(x_{ref})= \begin{bmatrix}e_{ref}\\e_{1,ref}\end{bmatrix} \longrightarrow \psi_{ref}=\phi(y_{ref}) \longrightarrow z_{ref}=\begin{bmatrix}\Re\psi_{ref}\\\Im\psi_{ref}\end{bmatrix}. \]

The controlled archives independently preserve this mapping. The 20 kPa result contains a 15001 x 2 observable-target array; the 40 kPa result contains a 30001 x 2 array. In both cases its names are exactly:

far_field_acoustic_echo
far_field_acoustic_echo_dot

Recomputing both columns from the archived state target agrees to 6.7e-16 for 20 kPa and 1.1e-15 for 40 kPa. This is numerical roundoff, so there is no ambiguity about what was supplied.

Both columns materially affect the lift. As a counterfactual sensitivity test, freezing autonomous echo-dot at its training center changes the settled target lift by 38.0% of its centered RMS at 20 kPa and 42.8% at 40 kPa. Freezing echo instead changes it by 112.0% and 115.8%, respectively. These are not additive importance fractions; they show that neither coordinate is ignored by the polynomial map.

The bottom-right panels show the quantities to which Q is actually applied: the errors in Re(psi) and Im(psi). The raw echo and echo-dot errors influence the controller through the nonlinear polynomial phi; they do not receive separate diagonal entries in Q.

9. Exactly how one control value is computed

For the current state, the same mappings are used:

\[ y=H(x),\qquad \psi=\phi(y),\qquad z=\begin{bmatrix}\Re\psi\\\Im\psi\end{bmatrix}. \]

For lambda=alpha+i beta, the one-mode split-real autonomous generator is

\[ A=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}. \]

The controller does not use a fitted constant input matrix. It recomputes the exact local lifted input direction from the current physical state:

\[ B_z(x)=\operatorname{realSplit}\!\left( \nabla_H\phi(H(x))\,J_H(x)\,g(x) \right). \]

This chain contains all three derivatives required by the change of variables:

  • g(x) says how pressure moves the RPE state;
  • J_H(x) says how a state displacement moves echo and autonomous echo-dot;
  • grad_H phi says how an echo-chart displacement moves the eigenfunction.

At every continuous BDF right-hand-side evaluation, the solver then solves

\[ A^TS+SA-SB_zR^{-1}B_z^TS+Q=0, \]

forms

\[ K(x)=R^{-1}B_z(x)^TS, \]

and returns

\[ u(\tau)=-K(x)\left[z(x)-z_{ref}(\tau)\right]. \]

For both runs, scalar Q=5000 becomes 5000 I_2. The 20 kPa case uses R=0.0001; the branch-stable 40 kPa case uses R=0.006.

The sequence is recomputed at internal adaptive trial states, not only at the 15,001 or 30,001 published output samples. Consequently, the saved control history is a sampled record of a feedback law that was evaluated much more often during integration.

10. Why the resulting pressure counts as rediscovery

The control equation contains no target pressure feedforward term. The solve job points to time and values in the target trajectory and does not name its stored control_values array. The known sine is loaded only by the later assessment scripts for plotting and error measurement.

This has an important consequence at the initial instant. If the controlled state equals the target state, then z-z_ref=0, so KLQR requests u=0, even though the target orbit is about to move under its hidden sine forcing. As the unforced controlled plant starts to separate from the moving target, feedback error develops and the controller generates the pressure needed to stay on the orbit. That mechanism explains both the acquisition transient and the fact that the recovered steady pressure is close to, but not identically equal to, the known sine.

The experiment is therefore stronger than fitting a sine to an echo record, but narrower than general blind input reconstruction. The target state history and exact plant/KROM derivatives are supplied; only the original driver history is withheld.

12. What this does and does not prove

The experiment establishes the following:

  • [e,L_f0 e] is a useful local coordinate chart on the trained branch.
  • A degree-four eigenfunction in that chart can approximately satisfy the selected Koopman generator relation.
  • Mapping both current and target states through that eigenfunction provides a two-real-state KLQR tracking signal.
  • Exact state-dependent lifted actuation can maintain held-out 20 and 40 kPa periodic orbits for long horizons.
  • The dominant feedback pressure can recover the withheld sine driver.

It does not yet implement an echo-only laboratory controller:

  1. The current simulated state (r,v) is used to evaluate H(x) and the exact B_z(x). No observer reconstructs these quantities from a sensor stream.
  2. The target is stored as a state trajectory and then mapped into observables. A laboratory target specified only as a desired echo waveform would need a compatible echo-dot target and a choice among any states sharing that output.
  3. L_f0 e must be separated from the total measured derivative under input. A sensor implementation needs an input-aware estimator or the correction L_f0e=de/dtau-L_ge\,u with enough information to evaluate L_g e.
  4. The controller has no pressure saturation, sensor noise, derivative filter, delay, parameter uncertainty, or transducer dynamics. The 20 kPa acquisition peak and 40 kPa correction spikes are not yet actuator-feasible.

The correct interpretation is therefore: this is a successful state-aware, exact-map validation of an eigenfunction whose coordinates are echo and autonomous echo-dot. It is strong evidence that the repaired representation is useful, but it is not yet a complete output-feedback laboratory realization.
