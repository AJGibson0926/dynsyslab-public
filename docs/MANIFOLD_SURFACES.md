# Manifold surface continuation

The explicit phase-space sidecar computes two-dimensional stable or unstable manifolds of autonomous hyperbolic saddle equilibria. Vertices retain every native state component (up to the sidecar's 64-component limit). Viewer shows a sparse, unfilled projection of accepted fronts only on state phase plots. Opening a plot remains a cache lookup; computing context is an explicit action.

The native implementation follows the boundary-value front construction described in section 2 of Krauskopf et al., *A survey of methods for computing (un)stable manifolds of vector fields* (2005), DOI [10.1142/S0218127405012533](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218127405012533). The author manuscript is available [here](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/2988488/2004r14.pdf). The Class Hub's reviewed invariance-equation record was consulted read-only. This is an independent implementation, with no copied code pack.

An initial eigenvector ellipse is advanced by shooting from the preceding ring into planes normal to its tangent. Time and the initial ring parameter satisfy the plane and outward-distance conditions. Excessive curvature reduces the step. Long or curved front edges require a fresh midpoint boundary-value solve. Accepted ribbons are triangulated. Distances use state coordinates divided by the declared domain widths; stored front distances are accumulated chord estimates, not exact geodesic distances.

Each result is recomputed with half the seed radius, radial step, and mesh spacing, doubled initial points, and tighter integration and chord tolerances. Full-state field tangency is checked at every triangle centroid. Geometric comparison covers the complete final boundary and distributed interior vertices/centroids. Failed rank, tangency, or refinement checks produce an unresolved result that Viewer omits. A resolved result covers the requested finite extent; truncated results preserve the last validated front and explain the domain, chart, or resource limit. Neither status certifies global completeness or an entire basin boundary.

The nested phase query option 'surface' supports 'enabled', 'initial_points', 'maximum_points', 'maximum_fronts', 'seed_radius', 'maximum_distance', 'step', 'mesh_size', 'chord_tolerance', and 'tangent_tolerance'. Defaults are visible in the API's normalized options. Fine validation can use four times the requested vertex budget and approximately twice the front budget. The cache method version is 3 and includes these options; previous caches remain intact.

Results contain 'manifold_surfaces': full-state 'vertices', zero-based vertex 'triangles' in triples, 'front_offsets' (first vertex of each closed ring; the last ends at vertex count), 'front_distances', the base equilibrium index, status/reason, and numerical diagnostics. Vertex zero is the equilibrium seed. There is no physical time coordinate on a surface mesh.

One-dimensional autonomous branches and periodic-forcing section curves remain supported. Higher-dimensional invariant sets, nonhyperbolic center manifolds, and two-dimensional forcing-section surfaces need different continuation methods and are reported as unsupported rather than represented by a tangent plane. Closed-front continuation can stop at the first domain boundary; it does not silently extrapolate the rest of the box.

Tests use exact curved invariant graphs in three and four dimensions, reversed stable dynamics, nonorthogonal complex eigenspaces, and the Lorenz stable surface. The papers are part of system research metadata and this documentation, not solution archives.
