# Hénon–Heiles analytic symmetry-axis orbits

The native system owns the family 'henon-heiles-straight-line' in addition to its harmonic lam=0 limit. It applies without forcing when position and momentum lie on the same one of the three symmetry axes. For q=lambda*s, the reduced equation is q''=-q+q² with energy E=q'²/2+q²/2-q³/3. The bounded well has 0<=E<=1/6. Escaping branches and generic off-axis motion are rejected.

For 0<E<1/6, the three potential roots q1<q2<q3 give q=q1+(q2-q1)sn²(a(t-t0)+z0|m), with a=sqrt((q3-q1)/6) and m=(q2-q1)/(q3-q1). The phase z0 is fixed by both initial position and velocity. At the homoclinic boundary the elementary sech-squared limit is evaluated without periodic wrapping. Both signs of nonzero lambda and all three axes use the same reduction.

The primary source is Brack, Mehta and Tanaka (2001), [author manuscript](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/1660/1/brack11.pdf), section 4, equations 27–33. Their scaled energy is e=6E. Their formulas for bifurcated orbits are not treated as exact general solutions of the native system. Independent tests check the native RHS, initial conditions, and convergence of numerical integration across all axes and signed lambda, including turning points and the separatrix.
